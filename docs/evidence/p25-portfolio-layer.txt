# GAP-007 slice 1 — the portfolio risk layer (correlation, equity-at-risk, cascade plan)

**Date:** 2026-10-13 · **Module:** `crates/sentinel-core/src/portfolio.rs`
**Tests:** `crates/sentinel-core/tests/portfolio_adversarial.rs` (11)

## What GAP-007 asks for

The evaluation's rank #7: isolated margin is per-position but *ruin* is
per-portfolio. Today five positions going Orange in the same tick fire five
independent reduces in state order, with no notion of which reduction buys the
most portfolio survival per dollar of fees and slippage. The blueprint lists
five parts; this slice delivers the first two as pure, tested primitives.

## What landed

**Correlation tracking.** EWMA mean, variance and pairwise covariance with
RiskMetrics-style decay (`lambda = 0.94`, ~60-observation half-life). Volatility
and correlation are `None` below `min_samples`, so the layer reports "not
measured" instead of a number it cannot support.

**Equity-at-risk.** `assess` weights each threatened position's collateral by
its modelled liquidation probability (`distance_to_liq / sigma` into a logistic
tail), so `equity_at_risk` is structurally `<= collateral_at_risk`. An
unmeasured market is treated as *likely* to liquidate, never as safe.

**Cascade planner.** `plan_cascade` ranks the book by

```text
ratio_i = (collateral_i * P(liq_i)) / (notional_i * cost_fraction)
          * (1 + mean correlation of i with the other threatened legs)
```

Budgets are consulted through the P24 `BudgetLedger::check`, which is pure, so
ranking candidates never consumes budget and refused positions land in an
explicit `denied` list.

## Three design failures the tests caught before they shipped

Worth recording, because each was a silent-wrong-answer bug rather than a crash.

1. **Double-scaled cross terms.** The EWMA covariance applied `(1 - lambda)` to
   the deviation *and* again in the update, understating every covariance by a
   factor of `(1 - lambda)` ≈ 0.06. Co-moving series measured a correlation near
   zero. Fixed by storing the deviation `r - mean_before` at observation time,
   which is the standard RiskMetrics form.

2. **Arrival-order pairing.** Pairing a return against the other market's *most
   recent* deviation compares returns from different times; feeding markets in
   batches reported `-0.18` for series that are identical. Rejected.

3. **A bounded per-market bucket window.** The first fix tried a window of 8
   buckets per market. Feeding 10 buckets of one market and then another evicted
   the first two before the partner arrived, dropping those pairs silently and
   understating the covariance by exactly the dropped fraction, measured as
   `rho = 0.809` on two identical series. **Any windowed design has this
   failure mode**, because the required window is a property of the caller's
   feed order, which the tracker cannot see.

The shipped API removes the class: `observe(bucket, &[(MarketId, Decimal)])`
takes **one time bucket per call**, with every market that printed in it. There
is nothing to pair across time and nothing to evict. A market that prints twice
in a bucket has its returns summed (log returns add over a period); a market
absent from the slice contributes no cross term, so a gap yields no information
rather than wrong information.

## The load-bearing test

`the_correlation_term_changes_the_order` is the one that decides whether this
module is a portfolio layer or a dressed-up collateral sort. Its fixture gives
SOL *more* collateral than ETH or BTC at the same notional, so a sort by
collateral-per-fee ranks SOL first; SOL is also the only leg that does not share
the book's exposure, so the planner must rank it **last**. The test asserts the
two orderings differ. They do.

## Status against the acceptance criterion

The blueprint's KPI is: *correlated-dump-class scenarios show >=20% less total
loss vs the per-position-independent baseline at equal fee spend.*

**Not measured yet, and therefore not claimed.** The planner is not wired into
the simulator: `sim/engine.rs::step` still evaluates positions in state order.
Wiring it is the next slice, and it is the one that produces the number. What
this slice establishes is that the ordering the wiring will apply is
correlation-aware and budget-honest, with the properties asserted rather than
assumed.

# GAP-007 slice 3 — the allocator (reserve + segregation), and why it still measured 0.00%

**Date:** 2026-10-13 · **Files:** `crates/sentinel-core/src/portfolio.rs`,
`crates/sentinel/src/sim/engine.rs`

## What was built

**Reserve** (`portfolio::book_watching`, `portfolio::reserve_actions`). While
the book *measures* as correlated (mean pairwise correlation at or above
`reserve_correlation`, at least `reserve_min_positions` positions), a share of
the day's action budget is held back from the reflex path so the cascade still
has an action when it fires. The reserve is never zero once engaged: a quarter
of a one-action budget floors to zero, which would hold nothing back exactly
when the budget is scarcest. During warmup the reserve is **off**, because
engaging it would hold actions back on the strength of a correlation nobody
measured.

**Segregation ([P-1]).** Each allowed market gets its own daily segment; the
book ceiling stays the policy's own value.

**Enforcement.** `Sim::portfolio_admits` runs ahead of the frozen
`PolicyEngine::evaluate` and only under `--cascade`, so the frozen gate and
every existing result are untouched. A refusal here is a budget decision, never
an executed action, so it cannot register as a policy violation.

Segregation verifiably works: the contention fixture records 18 refusals of the
form `BookDailyExhausted { cap: 1 } (reserve=0, segment=1)`.

## Measurement 1 — the contention fixture (cap = 1, two markets)

| run | capital saved | sentinel loss | liquidations avoided |
|---|---|---|---|
| no cascade | $66.3513 | $459.5877 | 0 |
| `--cascade` | $66.3513 | $459.5877 | 0 |

**Delta $0.00**, and the refusals explain why: `reserve=0`, because the first
single-position action happens at tick `1791854400000`, inside the tracker's
20-bucket warmup window. By the time the correlation is measurable the day's
single action is already spent.

This fixture is also degenerate by construction: with a one-action budget for
two markets, *whichever* market acts first wins, and no budget policy can change
that without deferring the first action — which is the barrier design, declined
below.

## Measurement 2 — the realistic suite (cap = 10)

| run | notional | capital saved | liq avoided | violations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no cascade | $38,533.50 | $394.02 | 1 | 0 |
| `--cascade` | $38,533.50 | $394.02 | 1 | 0 |

**Delta $0.00 again — and 0 of 12 scenarios recorded a single portfolio
denial.** Executed orders per scenario, against the cap of 10:

```text
rb-01 2   rb-02 2   rb-03 1   rb-04 2   rb-05 0   rb-06 4
rb-07 2   rb-08 1   rb-09 0   rb-10 2   rb-11 2   rb-12 4
max 4 / 10
```

## The actual finding

**The daily action budget is never scarce anywhere in the scenario set.** The
heaviest scenario spends 4 of 10 actions. When the resource being allocated is
never exhausted, every allocation mechanism — ordering, reservation,
segregation — is provably inert, because there is nothing to allocate. The
0.00% is not a defect in the planner; it is the scenario set failing to exercise
the mechanism.

This supersedes the slice-2 finding. Slice 2 said reordering is inert because
the budget is consumed before the cascade triggers. Slice 3 says allocation is
inert for a deeper reason: **the binding constraint is the test data, not the
architecture.** The blueprint's KPI (>=20% less total loss at equal fee spend)
is not *measurable* on the current scenario set under any mechanism.

## On the proposed two-phase barrier

Declined as specified, for a measurable reason. Deferring every single-position
reflex action to a cascade commit would hold protection on the 10-of-12
scenarios that are single-position or near it, in order to buy the rare
simultaneous case. The reflex path's value is acting immediately at
sub-microsecond latency on a local signal; a barrier spends that on a condition
that does not exist yet. The intent/commit split is kept where it costs
nothing — a reservation is mutable state, decoupled from execution — without
deferring execution itself.

A measured correction to the framing: this is not TOCTOU. The check and the
consume are atomic within one tick; there is no gap and no adversary. The
structure is that the reflex actor holds **global write scope** over the budget
while holding only **local read scope** over the book, and that the cascade
decision needs k >= 2 signals, so it is structurally *later* than any
single-signal decision. The distinction is operational: a TOCTOU remedy
(atomicity, locking) would not change the result, because check and use are
already atomic and the loss still happens. Reservation against a future class of
decisions does address it, and that is what was built.

## What the next slice should be

Not a better allocator. A **contention scenario family**: correlated
multi-position books whose daily actions genuinely exhaust the cap, so that
ordering, reservation and segregation have something to allocate. Without it
the KPI has no witness, and any percentage reported from the current set would
be measuring noise.

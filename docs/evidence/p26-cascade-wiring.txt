# GAP-007 slice 2 — the planner wired to the simulator, and the negative result it produced

**Date:** 2026-10-13 · **Files:** `crates/sentinel/src/sim/engine.rs`,
`crates/sentinel/src/bin/backtest.rs`

## What was wired

`--cascade` (opt-in, default off) adds a `PortfolioLayer` to the simulator:

- Returns are grouped **by timestamp** inside `walk`, so the tracker receives
  one bucket per instant with every market that printed in it, which is the API
  the portfolio module requires.
- `evaluation_order()` replaces the state-order loop when the plan triggers:
  positions at or above the trigger tier are ranked by the cascade plan and
  evaluated in that order, with everything else behind them.
- `BudgetLimits` is built from the **scenario's own policy config**, not from
  `Default`, and the ledger records every executed action, so the planner's
  budget query cannot drift from the gate the engine enforces.
- Each triggered tick journals a note: threatened count, equity-at-risk, the
  ranked order with ratios, and how many positions the budget refused.

Verified wiring: with a fixture whose daily cap is 1 and two threatened
positions, the plan appears 171 times and reads
`order=[16:285.7119] denied=1` — the correlated leg is admitted, the other is
refused by the budget, as designed.

## The result: zero effect, measured

Same fixture set, both runs, `--cascade` on and off:

| suite | notional | capital saved | sentinel liqs | baseline liqs | policy violations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| realistic, no cascade | $38,533.50 | $394.02 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| realistic, `--cascade` | $38,533.50 | $394.02 | 0 | 1 | 0 |

**Delta: $0.00 (0.0%).** Only 1 of 12 scenarios produced a plan at all, and the
one contention fixture designed to make ordering matter also produced $0.00.

## Why, at the mechanism level

The contention fixture (daily cap = 1, two positions) is the decisive one. Its
timeline:

- `t = 1791854400000` — market 32 goes Orange. Market 16 is still Green. **One**
  threatened position, so the cascade does not trigger; the reflex path acts on
  market 32 and consumes the single daily action.
- `t = 1791863670000` — market 16 goes Orange. Now two positions are threatened
  and the plan is computed on every tick thereafter. But the day's budget is
  already spent, so the gate refuses **both** positions regardless of order.

The ordering only changes an outcome when two or more actions compete for a
resource **at the moment of the plan**, and in this architecture the scarce
resource (the daily action) has already been consumed by an earlier
single-position event. Once it is gone, every ordering produces the same
outcome: nothing.

The general statement, which is the useful part: **in the current simulator the
per-position evaluations are independent and the shared budget is consumed
before the cascade triggers, so permuting the evaluation order is a no-op.** A
planner that only reorders cannot move the number.

## What the blueprint's KPI actually requires

The KPI is *correlated-dump-class scenarios show >=20% less total loss vs the
per-position-independent baseline at equal fee spend.* Reordering is not
sufficient to reach it. The version of GAP-007 that could reach it is an
**allocator**, not a scheduler: at the moment the cascade triggers it must
decide *how much* to cut on each leg and *reserve* budget for that decision,
rather than distributing an already-spent budget across an ordering.

That is a different piece of work with a different shape: a reserve/commit
protocol between the reflex path and the portfolio layer, plus slice sizing,
plus a policy for pre-empting a single-position action when the book looks
correlated. It is also the version that interacts with the `[P-1]` finding,
because a per-market budget is exactly what stops one market from spending the
day before the book needs it.

  changes the ordering.
- The wiring (slice 2) is real, verified by its own journal notes, and
  **measurably inert** on the current scenario set.
- The KPI is **not met**. The number reproduced is 0.0%, and this file is why.
- `--cascade` defaults to off, so every existing result is unchanged; the frozen

Recording a 0.0% instead of tuning the fixture until something moved is the
point. The useful output of this slice is the mechanism: ordering cannot help,
allocation can, and the next slice should be the allocator.
